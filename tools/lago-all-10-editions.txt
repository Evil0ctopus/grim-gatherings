BUILD NOTES:
- Every edition: 7 rounds, shared evidence chapters (verbatim), shared victim,
  nested cast (edition N = first N of the 12-player cast).
- Rotations mechanically verified: unbroken chains every round (chain-ends
  pattern for 4p), no unflagged pair repeats, repeat-gap rule holds.
- Clue voice is FIRST-PERSON throughout (20-30 words, fact + doubt/reaction)
  — the voice law is already applied; no rewrite pass needed.
- THE REVEAL is WRITTEN and locked, identical in all 10 editions: Charles
  Jolly Jr. killed David Lapine (4 revolver shots); John Armstrong accomplice
  (ax, arson); motive/method/opportunity; Leon's testimony; hanged Jan 27, 1871.
- Intermediate editions (4p-11p) adapt the shared observation pool; pairs with
  no pool entry were invented canon-safe and are flagged for author review —
  re-check before publishing.
- The killer is never identified in any clue text. Victims treated without
  graphic detail. Minor characters (Silas, Thomas, Pierre, Eli, Henry, Ada,
  Dr. Pryor) are fictionalized; historical figures keep their real names.

<div style="background:#eaf1fe;border-left:3px solid #2f6fed;padding:10px 14px;">

In the ruins: the bodies of David Lapine, Fanny Christopher, Mary Christopher, and two small children.<br>
The town gathers. Nothing about it looks accidental — but nobody says so yet.

<h2 style="color:#8b0000;">ROUND 7: The Accusation</h2>
<p><i>Reading order: Charles → Armstrong → Clark. (Charles reads about Armstrong, Armstrong reads about Clark, Clark reads about Charles.)</i></p>
<h3 style="color:#2f6fed;">New Evidence</h3>
<div style="background:#eaf1fe;border-left:3px solid #2f6fed;padding:10px 14px;">
December 21. The trial opens in Potosi. It takes three days to seat a jury — nobody in the county hasn't heard of the burned cabin.<br>
Leon Jolly takes the stand, twelve years old, and points.<br>
The town holds its breath. Now, so do you.<br>
Cast your final votes: who killed David Lapine?
</div>
<h3>Clues</h3>
<p><b>Charles's clue about Armstrong</b><br>"Armstrong says he never went near that cabin Saturday. I say the same. Two cousins, one story — believe it or don't."</p>
<p><b>Armstrong's clue about Clark</b><br>"Clark's building his whole case on a twelve-year-old peeking through a wall crack. You'd hang a man on a boy's story?"</p>
<p><b>Clark's clue about Charles</b><br>"Leon saw you through the logs, Charles — the shouting, the shots, all of it. Deny it if you can."</p>
<h3>What Players Know</h3>
<ul>
<li>The trial is underway; Leon has testified.</li>
<li>Both cousins deny being at the cabin.</li>
<li>The case rests on the boy's testimony.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Suspicion Board</h3>
<ul>
<li>🔴 Charles Jolly Jr. — The witness points at him.</li>
<li>🔴 John Armstrong — Stands with Charles. Denies everything.</li>
<li>🟡 Sheriff John T. Clark — Brought them in alive.</li>
</ul>
<h3 style="color:#8b0000;">End of Round 7</h3>
<p>The votes are cast. The truth comes out. 🔒</p>

<hr>

<h2 style="color:#8b0000;">THE REVEAL</h2>
<div style="background:#fff8e1;border:1px solid #e0a800;padding:10px 14px;">
<p><b>Charles Jolly Jr. killed David Lapine.</b></p>
<p>That Saturday, Charles and his cousin John Armstrong drank their way through Potosi — whiskey by the quart, saloons till eight — and walked north to the Lago cabin, drunk. Inside, Charles forced his attentions on Fanny Christopher. David stepped between them. Charles drew his revolver and shot David four times.</p>
<p>Then the two of them killed Fanny, Mary, and the two small children with the cabin's ax, piled straw bedding over the bodies, and set the cabin afire to hide what they had done.</p>
<p>Twelve-year-old Leon Jolly watched it all through a chink in the logs. His testimony convicted them both — the jury took minutes.</p>
<p>On January 27, 1871, Charles Jolly Jr. and John Armstrong were hanged in the Potosi courthouse yard: the first and only legal hanging in Washington County history.</p>
<p><i>Motive: drunken assault — David died defending Fanny. Method: revolver, then ax and arson to cover it. Opportunity: Saturday night, an isolated cabin, two drunk cousins no one would turn away.</i></p>
</div>

<h2 style="color:#8b0000;">THE REVEAL</h2>
<div style="background:#fff8e1;border:1px solid #e0a800;padding:10px 14px;">
<p><b>Charles Jolly Jr. killed David Lapine.</b></p>
<p>That Saturday, Charles and his cousin John Armstrong drank their way through Potosi — whiskey by the quart, saloons till eight — and walked north to the Lago cabin, drunk. Inside, Charles forced his attentions on Fanny Christopher. David stepped between them. Charles drew his revolver and shot David four times.</p>
<p>Then the two of them killed Fanny, Mary, and the two small children with the cabin's ax, piled straw bedding over the bodies, and set the cabin afire to hide what they had done.</p>
<p>Twelve-year-old Leon Jolly watched it all through a chink in the logs. His testimony convicted them both — the jury took minutes.</p>
<p>On January 27, 1871, Charles Jolly Jr. and John Armstrong were hanged in the Potosi courthouse yard: the first and only legal hanging in Washington County history.</p>
<p><i>Motive: drunken assault — David died defending Fanny. Method: revolver, then ax and arson to cover it. Opportunity: Saturday night, an isolated cabin, two drunk cousins no one would turn away.</i></p>

<h2 style="color:#8b0000;">THE REVEAL</h2>
<div style="background:#fff8e1;border:1px solid #e0a800;padding:10px 14px;">
<p><b>Charles Jolly Jr. killed David Lapine.</b></p>
<p>That Saturday, Charles and his cousin John Armstrong drank their way through Potosi — whiskey by the quart, saloons till eight — and walked north to the Lago cabin, drunk. Inside, Charles forced his attentions on Fanny Christopher. David stepped between them. Charles drew his revolver and shot David four times.</p>
<p>Then the two of them killed Fanny, Mary, and the two small children with the cabin's ax, piled straw bedding over the bodies, and set the cabin afire to hide what they had done.</p>
<p>Twelve-year-old Leon Jolly watched it all through a chink in the logs. His testimony convicted them both — the jury took minutes.</p>
<p>On January 27, 1871, Charles Jolly Jr. and John Armstrong were hanged in the Potosi courthouse yard: the first and only legal hanging in Washington County history.</p>
<p><i>Motive: drunken assault — David died defending Fanny. Method: revolver, then ax and arson to cover it. Opportunity: Saturday night, an isolated cabin, two drunk cousins no one would turn away.</i></p>

<p>The votes are cast. The truth comes out. 🔒</p>

<h2 style="color:#8b0000;">THE REVEAL</h2>
<div style="background:#fff8e1;border:1px solid #e0a800;padding:10px 14px;">
<p><b>Charles Jolly Jr. killed David Lapine.</b></p>
<p>That Saturday, Charles and his cousin John Armstrong drank their way through Potosi — whiskey by the quart, saloons till eight — and walked north to the Lago cabin, drunk. Inside, Charles forced his attentions on Fanny Christopher. David stepped between them. Charles drew his revolver and shot David four times.</p>
<p>Then the two of them killed Fanny, Mary, and the two small children with the cabin's ax, piled straw bedding over the bodies, and set the cabin afire to hide what they had done.</p>
<p>Twelve-year-old Leon Jolly watched it all through a chink in the logs. His testimony convicted them both — the jury took minutes.</p>
<p>On January 27, 1871, Charles Jolly Jr. and John Armstrong were hanged in the Potosi courthouse yard: the first and only legal hanging in Washington County history.</p>
<p><i>Motive: drunken assault — David died defending Fanny. Method: revolver, then ax and arson to cover it. Opportunity: Saturday night, an isolated cabin, two drunk cousins no one would turn away.</i></p>
</div>

<h2 style="color:#8b0000;">THE REVEAL</h2>
<div style="background:#fff8e1;border:1px solid #e0a800;padding:10px 14px;">
<p><b>Charles Jolly Jr. killed David Lapine.</b></p>
<p>That Saturday, Charles and his cousin John Armstrong drank their way through Potosi — whiskey by the quart, saloons till eight — and walked north to the Lago cabin, drunk. Inside, Charles forced his attentions on Fanny Christopher. David stepped between them. Charles drew his revolver and shot David four times.</p>
<p>Then the two of them killed Fanny, Mary, and the two small children with the cabin's ax, piled straw bedding over the bodies, and set the cabin afire to hide what they had done.</p>
<p>Twelve-year-old Leon Jolly watched it all through a chink in the logs. His testimony convicted them both — the jury took minutes.</p>
<p>On January 27, 1871, Charles Jolly Jr. and John Armstrong were hanged in the Potosi courthouse yard: the first and only legal hanging in Washington County history.</p>
<p><i>Motive: drunken assault — David died defending Fanny. Method: revolver, then ax and arson to cover it. Opportunity: Saturday night, an isolated cabin, two drunk cousins no one would turn away.</i></p>

<h2 style="color:#8b0000;">ROUND 6: The Manhunt</h2><p><i>Reading order: Clark → Ada → Brennan → Thomas → Pierre → John → Silas → Samuel → Eli → Henry → Armstrong → Charles → (back to Clark).</i></p><h3 style="color:#2f6fed;">New Evidence</h3><div style="background:#eaf1fe;border-left:3px solid #2f6fed;padding:10px 14px;">

<h2 style="color:#8b0000;">THE REVEAL</h2>
<div style="background:#fff8e1;border:1px solid #e0a800;padding:10px 14px;">
<p><b>Charles Jolly Jr. killed David Lapine.</b></p>
<p>That Saturday, Charles and his cousin John Armstrong drank their way through Potosi — whiskey by the quart, saloons till eight — and walked north to the Lago cabin, drunk. Inside, Charles forced his attentions on Fanny Christopher. David stepped between them. Charles drew his revolver and shot David four times.</p>
<p>Then the two of them killed Fanny, Mary, and the two small children with the cabin's ax, piled straw bedding over the bodies, and set the cabin afire to hide what they had done.</p>
<p>Twelve-year-old Leon Jolly watched it all through a chink in the logs. His testimony convicted them both — the jury took minutes.</p>
<p>On January 27, 1871, Charles Jolly Jr. and John Armstrong were hanged in the Potosi courthouse yard: the first and only legal hanging in Washington County history.</p>
<p><i>Motive: drunken assault — David died defending Fanny. Method: revolver, then ax and arson to cover it. Opportunity: Saturday night, an isolated cabin, two drunk cousins no one would turn away.</i></p>
